# Cadence — positioning

> Product-positioning and market rationale, kept out of `product-brief.md` on purpose: this
> material justifies the product to a human reader (pitch, README, project defense) but yields no
> testable requirement, so it does not belong in the agent-facing brief. Companion to
> [`docs/product-brief.md`](product-brief.md).

## One-line differentiator

A time tracker with the analytics depth and coaching of a recovery wearable, kept dead-simple to
operate.

## Target segments

All share one job: run several parallel efforts with no external structure forcing them to track,
and improve — not just record.

- **Primary — the multi-project builder.** A main product plus side projects, client work, a
  course, a language. Wants to know where hours go, whether work is regular or bursty, and how to
  get more effective. Intrinsically motivated, data-comfortable, often already wears a Whoop/Oura.
- **Indie hackers / solo founders** — building + marketing + sales + learning, zero external
  accountability, need a mirror on their own effort.
- **Freelancers / consultants with several clients** — constant context-switching; want per-project
  hour insight for self-management (not billing).
- **Students & self-learners on parallel tracks** — courses, languages, side projects; want
  consistency and visible progress-in-hours. Deliberate practice is the ethos.
- **Researchers / PhDs / creatives** — long unstructured days across writing, reading, experiments;
  want to see focus vs. fragmentation.
- **Employed people with serious side projects** — a few hours a day that must count; consistency
  tracking is the lever.

## Anti-users (explicitly not the target)

Teams needing shared timesheets; people who want passive/automatic tracking; anyone needing
invoicing/billing; those wanting a full task/project manager. Single-user, intentional,
insight-first by design.

## Why not an existing tool

- **Toggl / Clockify** — tracking + reports, billing-oriented; no baseline-relative scoring, no
  coaching. They tell you the hours, not whether the week was good.
- **RescueTime** — automatic/passive tracking. Cadence deliberately rejects this: tracking is
  **manual because the act of pressing start *is* the deliberate-practice signal**. Passive tools
  tell you what happened; Cadence makes you decide what you are doing.
- **Whoop / Oura** — the model, but for the body. Cadence ports their pattern — score against your
  own rolling baseline, zone it (green/yellow/red), coach it — to work time, where nothing
  comparable exists in a tool this simple.

## The core bet

For self-directed people, the missing feedback loop is not "how many hours" (every tracker answers
that) but "how well, versus my own normal, and what to change." Wearables proved that turning raw
signal into baseline-relative scores plus short coaching changes behavior. Cadence applies that loop
to work time while keeping operation to start / stop / pause.
